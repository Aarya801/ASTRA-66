§0Scope & safety boundary

Propulsion is outside this design
IN SCOPE
  • Airframe, structures, fins, recovery bay and attachment points
  • Sensing, logging and telemetry electronics (no outputs that fire anything)
  • Analysis, drawings, test plan, and the gates that lead to a supervised flight
OUT OF SCOPE — NEVER FABRICATED
  • Motors, propellant, ejection charges, igniters, pyrotechnics
  • Motor interface dimensions: left as parameters to be taken from the maker's current documents
  • Electronic deployment: recovery uses the certified motor's own ejection, prepared by a mentor

This package covers a complete, manufacturable airframe. Motor mass, length and retainer geometry appear only as clearly marked placeholders so that the centre of gravity (CG) can be computed. When your mentor has selected the certified motor, copy its published data into simulation/motor_config.json. Set status to MANUFACTURER_DATA and record the source, verifier and date, then re-run python cad/build_cad.py.

§2Design objectives

Flight & safety

  • Static margin 1.5–3.0 cal from rail exit to burnout, confirmed by measured CG
  • Recovery-train hardware rated at ≥ 2× the estimated deployment load
  • No energetic material is designed, stored in, or controlled by the airframe
  • Subsonic envelope (≤ 90 m/s) matched to printed and paper-tube structures

Build & cost

  • Uses only a hobby FDM printer, hand tools, and an optional laser-cutting service
  • Metric ISO fasteners and heat-set inserts: no machining
  • Low-cost build under $232–437, excluding motors
  • Every interface is parameterised, so a tube-size change only needs a rebuild

Service & demonstration

  • Fins replaceable in minutes (slide-in, locked by a screwed ring)
  • Nose, hatch and aft bulkhead give tool-light access to every compartment
  • Electronics sled can be removed and bench-tested on a harness
  • Exhibition-ready: cutaway drawings, live telemetry demo, display stand

§3System architecture

Four airframe modules join at numbered interfaces. Only interface I-03 separates in flight. Thrust enters through the commercial retainer (I-05) and passes forward along the motor mount tube, centering rings and body tube. Recovery loads run from the eyebolt, through the tether, to the aramid anchor bonded to the motor mount.

§4CAD architecture

One parameter set drives everything. analysis/analysis.py holds the values, and build.py writes them to cad/astra66_params.scad. Every OpenSCAD source includes that file:

  • the master assembly cad/assembly/astra66_assembly.scad;
  • one file per part in cad/parts/;
  • the station library cad/lib/astra66_core.scad.
Changing a length or diameter moves every dependent feature. cad/build_cad.py renders, validates and exports the whole model; the results are in documentation/CAD_VALIDATION.md.

Modelling rules

  • Datum: rocket axis = +Z; station 0 at the nose tip. Every part is modelled in assembly coordinates, so there are no mating constraints to break.
  • Naming: modules use nose_base(), fincan_core() and so on. Part numbers appear in comments and in the STL filenames.
  • Fits: all clearances are named parameters (FIT_SH, FIN_SLOT_CLR, FIN_GUIDE_CLR). Print a coupon first, then adjust.
  • External parts are transparent % envelopes: motor, retainer, rail buttons, eyebolt. Their sizes are COMMERCIAL SPEC parameters.
  • Outputs: the pipeline writes print-oriented STLs to cad/exports/stl, laser-cut DXF/SVG profiles to cad/exports/dxf, and drawings made from true sections of the rendered meshes to cad/drawings. cad/astra66.scad keeps the rev A PART= selector.
  • Porting: analysis/results/parameters.csv maps 1:1 onto a FreeCAD spreadsheet, Onshape variables, or Fusion user parameters if you rebuild in a feature-based CAD tool.

§5Engineering assumptions

Geometry & materials

  • The nominal 66.0 / 64.0 mm tube and 63.8 / 61.0 mm coupler must be measured when bought. ASSUMPTION
  • Densities: paper tube 1.0, PETG 1.27, ASA 1.07, birch ply 0.68 g/cm³. ASSUMPTION
  • Allowables are deliberately low: 1 MPa for paper-tube bonds and 40 MPa for ply bending. ASSUMPTION
  • Electronics, camera and recovery masses are planning values: weigh everything. USER-SUPPLIED

Aerodynamics & flight

  • Barrowman method: subsonic, small angle of attack, thin fins, no body lift. ASSUMPTION
  • Design axial load factor 15 g, until replaced by 1.5× the simulated peak acceleration. ASSUMPTION
  • Parachute Cd 0.8 and opening-shock factor 1.8. ASSUMPTION
  • Motor 125 mm / 110 g loaded / 55 g burnout are placeholders, not a real product. COMMERCIAL SPEC required
MUST BE EXPERIMENTALLY VALIDATED OR MENTOR-REVIEWED BEFORE FLIGHTMeasured CG with the actual motor installed · OpenRocket stability with weighed masses · motor, retainer, delay and igniter choice (mentor/RSO) · separation force at I-03 · recovery hardware ratings and static proof load · fin alignment · heat tolerance of printed parts near the motor · radio legality · the range's own safety code and site limits.

7.2 Centre of gravity, centre of pressure, static margin

CG = Σ(mᵢ·xᵢ) / Σmᵢ → liftoff STA 696 · burnout 678 · no motor 657 mm Nose (tangent ogive): CNα = 2 X_N = 0.466·L_N = 123.0 mm Fins (Barrowman): L_F = √(S² + (X_R + C_T/2 − C_R/2)²) = 65.0 mm CNα_F = [1 + R/(S+R)] · 4N(S/d)² / [1 + √(1 + (2L_F/(C_R+C_T))²)] = 8.23 (K_FB = 1.355) X_F = X_B + (X_R/3)(C_R+2C_T)/(C_R+C_T) + (1/6)[(C_R+C_T) − C_R·C_T/(C_R+C_T)] = 1,052 mm CP = (2·X_N + CNα_F·X_F) / (2 + CNα_F) → STA 870 mm SM = (CP − CG) / d → liftoff 2.63 cal · burnout 2.92 cal · no motor 3.23 cal

The margin grows as propellant burns, because the CG moves forward. The worst case is therefore at liftoff and rail exit, which is the case to confirm with the real motor. The chart shows how sensitive the margin is to motor mass. Heavier motors in the same class are usually also longer, which moves the CG further aft than this chart models.

At 320 g the model still gives 1.75 cal. Use the NC-104 ballast stack if a measured CG moves the margin below 1.5 cal.

7.4 Nose-cone geometry rationale

  • Tangent ogive, fineness 4.0: below Mach 0.3, skin friction dominates drag, and ogive, elliptical and Haack shapes differ by only a few percent. The ogive has a simple closed-form CP (0.466 L), is supported directly by OpenRocket, and looks right for an aerospace-style vehicle.
  • ρ = 1,072.5 mm. The tip half-angle of 14.3° means the shell prints tip-up with no support. Wall thickness is three 0.4 mm perimeters.
  • The internal volume holds the ballast stack, well forward where each gram of ballast shifts the CG most.

7.5 Aerodynamic configuration

L/D of 17.3 with a continuous 66 mm body and no transitions. Surface steps are limited to the switch band joints, the lens cowl (8 mm high, streamlined) and the rail buttons. A small asymmetric side force from the cowl and buttons is expected; it is negligible at this margin, but OpenRocket should include the buttons. Fin edges are fully rounded, not airfoiled: the drag penalty is small at M < 0.3, and rounded edges are easy to reproduce.

7.7 Vibration considerations

  • Uniform-beam estimate of the first free–free bending mode: ≈ 48 Hz (EI from the assumed tube modulus). This is an upper bound, because joints reduce it. Treat it as an order of magnitude only.
  • Main excitation sources: motor combustion roughness, rail-exit shock, fin buffeting, and the deployment snatch. The biggest avionics risks are connectors backing out and the battery shifting.
  • Mitigations: locking connectors (JST-GH), wires tied down within 20 mm of every solder joint, battery in a cage plus strap, nylock nuts, no threadlocker on plastic. Hard-mount the IMU; rubber isolation would distort its data. The SD card is retained with tape.
  • Test: a hand shake/drop test with the logger running, then check the log for dropouts (§12).

Ground-station telemetry concept

  • Downlink packet at 2–5 Hz: time, state, baro altitude, vertical speed, accel magnitude, GPS lat/lon/fix, battery voltage, breakwire.

  • Full-rate data (≥ 100 Hz IMU) goes to microSD only; the radio carries summaries.

Integration rules

  • Keep the GPS antenna in the payload bay, with its sky view forward and away from the LoRa antenna and battery.
  • Use keyed connectors at the I-08 grommet so the sled comes out without desoldering.
  • Arm on the pad with the switch-band switch; the buzzer confirms the state.
  • Seal the barometer from light and draughts with open-cell foam; size static ports per the sensor maker's guidance.
  • Use a radio band and power level legal in your country only.

§10Manufacturing plan

  1. Measure purchased stock. Measure tube, coupler and MMT diameters at 3 stations × 2 axes; weigh 100 mm offcuts. Update analysis/analysis.py and run build.py.
  2. Print fit coupons. Print 10 mm-tall shoulder rings at −0.1, −0.2, −0.3 and −0.4 mm, plus a guide-slot coupon and an insert coupon. Choose the values and set FIT_SH and FIN_GUIDE_CLR.
  3. Print jigs first. Print GS-701 fin jig, a wrap-around tube marking guide (slots, holes at 45°/90°/270°) and a tube-cutting collar.
  4. Cut tubes. PL-201, AV-302 and BO-401 from body stock; AV-301 from coupler stock; BO-402 from MMT stock. Square each end on a sanding plate.
  5. Machine tube features. Cut the four fin slots with a fine saw and file to width using a fin offcut as a gauge. Drill the nose-screw, rail-button, lens, switch and static-port holes through the guide. Cut the hatch opening only after bonding PL-206.
  6. Plywood parts. Laser-cut AV-303/304 (2 discs each), BO-404, BO-405, and 5 fins (1 spare) from the DXFs in cad/exports/dxf. Knife-cut the AV-309 gasket. Laminate the bulkhead discs with epoxy under weight.
  7. Printed parts. Print from the ready-oriented STLs in cad/exports/stl. The pose and support needs of each part are listed in documentation/CAD_VALIDATION.md §4.9.
    • PETG: NC-101, NC-102, NC-103, PL-202–206, AV-306, AV-308, AV-312, BO-409.
    • ASA (enclosed printer): BO-403, PL-204.
    Install heat-set inserts while the parts are fresh.
  8. Fin finishing. Radius the leading and trailing edges (R1.5), seal the edges with thin epoxy, and sand. Keep the tabs bare and check each fin in the guide coupon.
  9. Inspection. Check every part against the tolerance table (7.10) and record results in the inspection log before assembly.

Manufacturing notes

  • Dry PETG/ASA before printing; wet filament weakens layer bonding.
  • Orient printed parts so loads cross layers along their length: core rails are printed vertically; sled flat.
  • Epoxy: sand both surfaces (120 grit), wipe with isopropyl alcohol, weigh the mix ratio, and record the batch and date.
  • Round edges on paper-tube slots and seal them with thin CA or epoxy to stop delamination.
  • Keep paint light; each 10 g aft costs about 0.05 cal of margin.

Tools required

  • FDM 3D printer (≥ 220 × 220 × 250 mm build volume), PETG + ASA capable (enclosure for ASA)
  • Soldering iron with heat-set insert tips; digital calipers (0.01 mm)
  • Fine-tooth saw or tube cutter + printed tube-cutting guide; hand files, sanding blocks
  • Drill + brad-point bits; hole saw or laser-cutting service for ply parts
  • Digital kitchen scale (0.1 g, 2 kg), spring/luggage scale for pull tests
  • Digital angle gauge, square, straight-edge / aluminium angle
  • Nitrile gloves, respirator for sanding and ASA printing, safety glasses
  • Multimeter, USB-serial adapter, crimp tool for JST connectors

§11Assembly procedure

Booster (400)

  1. Dry-fit BO-402 MMT, BO-404, BO-403 and fins inside BO-401, with GS-701 on the tail. Mark the ring positions.
  2. Wrap the BO-410 aramid leader around the MMT between the ring stations, saturate it with epoxy, and route it through the BO-404 notch.
  3. Bond BO-404 at the top of the MMT and BO-403 at the aft end, with fillets on the forward faces. Keep glue out of the fin guides.
  4. Insert the fins into the guides and slide the whole motor-mount assembly into BO-401, using the fins in the tube slots as keys. Bond the rings to the tube wall. Tape the jig on and leave to cure.
  5. Remove the fins. Bond BO-409 against the BO-404 forward face at 45°. Install both rail buttons and check them against a straight-edge.
  6. Bond the commercial retainer BO-407 exactly per the maker's instructions and have a mentor inspect it.
  7. Refit the fins, fit BO-405, and tighten 4× M3 × 12. Check cant and spacing (7.10).

Forward section (100–300)

  1. Bond NC-101 to NC-102 on a V-block, with the seam flush. Fit NC-103 and the NC-104 rod.
  2. Bond PL-206 inside PL-201, then cut the hatch opening. Bond PL-203 (camera in place for alignment) and PL-204.
  3. Install the M4 inserts in AV-303 and bond it into the forward end of AV-301.
  4. Bond AV-302 at the centre of AV-301 with the coupler inserted into offcuts on both sides for alignment. Drill the switch and static-port holes.
  5. Bond the AV-301 forward half into PL-201 (I-02), with the switch hole aligned on the 90° line.
  6. Bolt PL-202 to AV-303 with 2× M3 × 16 from inside the bay into the foot inserts. Route the harness through the grommet and seal around it with putty.
  7. Fit rods, then the forward AV-312 spacers, the sled (modules on standoffs, battery cage screwed from below), the aft spacers, the AV-309 gasket, AV-304 with its eyebolt, and a washer + nylock nut on each rod.
  8. Attach the nose with 3× M3 × 8. Tune the I-03 fit with sanding or a single wrap of tape until it meets the separation-force target.

§13Simulation workflow (OpenRocket)

  1. Build the component tree, top to bottom: Nose cone → Body tube "Payload" → Body tube "Switch band" (inner tube "AV coupler" + bulkheads) → Body tube "Booster" (inner tube "MMT", centering rings, fin set, rail buttons, parachute, shock cord, mass components).
  2. Enter geometry from the table below. Set every length relative to its parent so it matches the CAD stations.
  3. Enter masses: first as materials, then replace each component with its weighed mass (override mass). Add mass components for electronics, battery, camera, GPS and ballast at their stations.
  4. Compare with this package: with no motor, OpenRocket's CG should be within ±10 mm of STA 657 and its CP within ±15 mm of STA 870. Investigate any bigger difference before continuing.
  5. Motor configuration: with your mentor, choose certified motors from OpenRocket's built-in database (sourced from ThrustCurve) that fit the 29 mm mount and your range's limits. Add each ejection-delay option as its own configuration.
  6. Launch conditions: the range's rail length and angle, site altitude and temperature, and wind at 0, 10 and 15 km/h.
  7. Read and record: stability at rail exit (≥ 1.5 cal), rail-exit velocity, max velocity (≤ 90 m/s), max acceleration (≤ 15 g), apogee, optimum delay vs available delay, deployment velocity (≤ 20 m/s), descent rate, ground-hit velocity, landing distance.
  8. Iterate: if any limit fails, change the airframe (ballast, fins) or pick a different certified motor. Do not alter a motor. Re-run build.py so the drawings stay in step.
  9. Cross-check (optional): RASAero II for drag and CP at Mach 0.1–0.3; Monte-Carlo in OpenRocket for wind and mass scatter.
