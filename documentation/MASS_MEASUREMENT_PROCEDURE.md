# ASTRA-66 — Mass and centre-of-gravity measurement procedure

> **No mass in this repository has been measured.** Every value in `analysis/analysis.py` is CALCULATED (CAD volume ×
> assumed density × fill) or an ASSUMPTION, and the CG and static margin follow from them. This procedure defines how
> to record real measurements so the existing analysis consumes them. Do not enter a value that was not measured, and
> do not adjust a measurement to match a prediction. The project stays NOT FLIGHT CERTIFIED.

## 1. Reference datum and conventions

| Convention | Definition |
|---|---|
| Datum (STA 0) | The nose tip, on the rocket axis. Stations increase **aft**; the airframe ends at STA 1144 mm |
| Station of a part | Distance from the datum to the part's own centre of gravity, along the axis |
| Mass unit | grams (g), one decimal |
| Length unit | millimetres (mm), one decimal |
| Orientation | Record the orientation used for every CG measurement (nose up / horizontal) |

Existing station values are in `analysis/results/mass_budget.csv` (column `cg_station_mm`) and, for the avionics items,
in `avionics/integration/avionics_mass_properties.csv`.

## 2. Equipment

- Scale: resolution ≤ 0.1 g for parts, ≤ 1 g for the assembled rocket; record make, model and resolution.
- Calibration check before each session with a known mass; record the check result.
- Steel rule or calliper for stations (≤ 0.5 mm resolution), and a knife-edge or a round bar for the balance method.
- Two people for the assembled-vehicle measurements.

## 3. Individual component mass

Weigh every item **as it will fly**: printed parts after cleaning and any paint, boards with headers, connectors and
the wiring that belongs to them, fasteners in their sets.

Record one row per item:

| Field | Example / rule |
|---|---|
| `id` | the existing part or module id (`AV-306`, `EL-AV`, `EL-BAT`, …) so it maps onto the mass budget |
| `description` | what was actually weighed, including what was attached |
| `mass_g` | measured value, one decimal |
| `uncertainty_g` | scale resolution, or the spread if weighed more than once |
| `station_mm` | measured or CAD station of the item's CG (state which) |
| `date`, `operator` | when and who |
| `notes` | anything unusual (wet paint, missing screw, substituted part) |

Weigh sub-assemblies that are hard to separate as one item and say so, instead of splitting the mass by guesswork.

## 4. Assembled avionics mass

1. Assemble the sled exactly as it will fly: boards, standoffs, wiring, battery, cage, switch, breakwire tails.
2. Weigh the complete sled assembly (`EL-AV` + `AV-306` + `AV-308` + `EL-BAT` + fasteners) as one number.
3. Weigh the payload-bay electronics (`EL-GPS`, tray, camera if fitted) as one number.
4. Compare each total with the sum of the individual items from §3: they should agree within the summed uncertainty.
   If they do not, find the missing item before entering anything.

## 5. Total dry mass

Dry mass = the complete vehicle ready to fly **without the motor** and without the recovery charge (there is none in
this design: the certified motor's own ejection is used). Weigh:

1. Each module separately (nose, payload bay, avionics bay, booster) with everything installed.
2. The fully assembled vehicle, motor tube empty.
3. Note what is not yet fitted (paint, decals, ballast) and repeat when it is.

## 6. Centre of gravity

**Balance method (primary).**

1. Rest the assembled vehicle horizontally on a knife-edge or a round bar, fins clear of the surface.
2. Slide until it balances; mark the point and measure its distance from the nose tip (the datum): that is the CG
   station. Repeat three times and record all three readings, not just the mean.
3. Repeat with the motor casing fitted **only when a mentor is present and the motor is inert / not loaded**, and
   record it as a separate line. A loaded motor is handled by a certified person only.

**Moment method (cross-check).**

Support the vehicle at two points a known distance apart, weigh each support, and compute the CG from the moments.
Agreement with the balance method within a few millimetres is expected; record both.

## 7. Entering measurements into the analysis

The analysis is the single source of truth; measured values replace assumptions there, and everything downstream
(mass budget, CG, static margin, simulation, CAD mass cross-check) is regenerated by the pipeline.

1. Edit the relevant `add(...)` line in `analysis/analysis.py`: put the measured mass in place of the assumption and
   set its provenance tag to `USER` (the project's tag for a value you supply, "measured part"; the tags are `CALC`,
   `ASM`, `USER` and `COTS`, defined at the top of that file). Put the date, the scale and the measured uncertainty in
   the note argument, e.g. `"weighed 2026-10-01, 0.1 g scale, 58.4 +/- 0.1 g"`, so the mass budget shows where the
   number came from.
2. If a part's CG station was measured rather than taken from the CAD, update the station argument too.
3. Re-run the pipeline: `python run_validation.py`. It regenerates `analysis/results/mass_budget.csv`, the CG and
   static margin, the simulation results and the CAD ↔ avionics mass report.
4. Check the results that matter:
   - liftoff CG and static margin against the 1.5–3.0 calibre target (`documentation/ENGINEERING_STATUS.md`);
   - the recomputed CG against the measured CG from §6: they should agree within the measurement uncertainty. A
     disagreement means an item is missing or mis-stationed — investigate, do not tune the numbers;
   - the avionics table `avionics/integration/avionics_mass_properties.csv` for the effect of each item on the CG.
5. Record the measurement session (who, when, scale, results) alongside the bench records so the change is traceable.

## 8. What stays open after this procedure

- Static margin with the **certified motor** fitted: needs the motor's published mass and the measured CG
  (**mentor review**).
- Any mass that could not be weighed (for example a part not yet made) stays an assumption and must remain tagged as
  one.
- Measured masses do not make the design flight certified: the flight-readiness review and range approval are separate.
